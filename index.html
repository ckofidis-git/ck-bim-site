About

Christos Kofidis

Christos is a Chartered Construction Manager (MCIOB) with an MEng in Civil & Structural Engineering and more than 15 years' experience in BIM and digital construction, applied on major live programmes in the UK, the Middle East and Greece.

His career spans aviation, national high-speed rail, nuclear new build, water infrastructure and new-city development. Across these sectors the objective is the same: the right information, for the right people, in a form they can act on.

He believes effective information management should be largely invisible to the teams it serves: coordination runs smoothly, reviews are timely and nothing is missed. Achieving this requires a clear strategy, consistent standards and the systematic automation of repetitive tasks.

Workshop

Digital tools and workflows

Christos develops in-house tools for BIM and GIS delivery. The summaries below describe, at a high level, how each one works and the value it delivers. The tools are not distributed, and the projects, clients and data they support remain confidential.

BIM · Model review

Automated model review

Applies one consistent review standard to every design team in a single automated run.

  1. Configure. The reviewer selects the package and design stage, and the corresponding rules are loaded automatically.
  2. Check. Each submitted model is assessed against the client's model-quality checklist and the information delivery plan for that stage.
  3. Report. The output is a scored report for each model and a draft comment register for the reviewer to verify before issue.

Consistent, repeatable reviews across building and infrastructure models.

BIM · Quantities

Model-to-bill linking

Links model quantities to the bill of quantities, item by item.

  1. Interpret. Bill descriptions written by different teams are normalised into consistent, structured items.
  2. Match. Each modelled element is assigned to its priced item using its size, depth and the project's standard details.
  3. Reconcile. Model quantities are compared with the bill on a dashboard, with every discrepancy listed.

Cost and model data that reconcile.

BIM · Data

Model data extraction

Makes model content and quantities available to stakeholders without authoring software.

  1. Convert. Submitted models are converted to structured data in a single batch.
  2. Analyse. Element counts, quantities and data gaps are calculated for each model.
  3. Share. A single dashboard consolidates every package, milestone and discipline.

Package-level insight accessible to the whole team.

BIM · 4D

Lightweight 4D sequencing

Enables construction sequence reviews without specialist software.

  1. Load. Open-format exports of the design models are combined in a single 3D scene.
  2. Plan. A construction programme is defined and model elements are linked to its activities.
  3. Simulate. The sequence is played against a timeline, making sequencing conflicts as visible as spatial clashes.

Sequencing reviewed as part of routine coordination.

GIS · Integration

BIM-to-GIS pipeline

Brings design models and as-built records into GIS through a consistent, repeatable process.

  1. Import. Design models, as-built drawings and survey data are loaded into a single geodatabase.
  2. Classify. Features are organised by system and cleaned using rule tables rather than manual editing.
  3. Publish. The map is symbolised and arranged, and field packages are validated before release.

A GIS that remains aligned with the models.

GIS · Register

BIM × GIS register

Shows which models are mapped, which are out of date and which are missing.

  1. Collect. Map layers, the model register and the deliverables tracker are compared side by side.
  2. Compare. Submissions not yet mapped and models missing from the tracker are flagged.
  3. Report. A single dashboard reports the status of every project, with a portable copy for distribution.

GIS and model records kept in step.

Data · Reviews

Review-comment analytics

Measures how design reviews influence the design.

  1. Trace. Each comment is traced across resubmissions, from first issue to close-out.
  2. Measure. Resolved, recurring and high-value comments are quantified.
  3. Report. Results are presented in a chart report for leadership.

Review effort focused where it adds most value.
